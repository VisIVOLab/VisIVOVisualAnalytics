VisIVO VR privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026

This policy covers the VisIVO VR app for Meta Quest headsets (package it.inaf.oact.visivo.vr), the public loader page at https://vr.visivolab.oact.inaf.it/ and the pairing service behind it. They are published by VisIVOLab, INAF – Osservatorio Astrofisico di Catania, Italy, as part of the open-source VisIVO Visual Analytics software.

In short. The headset's camera is used only on the headset, to read the pairing QR code shown by VisIVO Visual Analytics on your computer. No camera image is stored or sent anywhere. The pairing service sees the pairing code and connection details for the few seconds it takes to connect your headset to your computer. Your data (cubes, images, catalogues) go from your own VisIVO backend to your headset over an encrypted connection and never through our servers. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

The camera

  • When you open the app, it asks for permission to use the headset's passthrough camera (android.permission.CAMERA and horizonos.permission.HEADSET_CAMERA). The only use of the camera is to read the QR code on your computer's screen.
  • Camera frames are decoded on the headset, in memory, while the scanner is open. Only the text of the QR code is used. No image or video is saved, and none leaves the headset. The app itself has no permission to use the network: the VR pages are opened by the headset's own browser component, which never receives a camera frame.
  • The camera stops when a code is read, when you close the scanner, and when you leave it.
  • You can refuse the permission, or withdraw it at any time in the headset's settings (Settings → Privacy & safety → App permissions). The app still works: you can type the code shown on your computer instead.

Pairing your headset with your computer

  • Pairing uses a short code that VisIVO Visual Analytics shows on your computer. The pairing service, run by INAF on the loader's server, uses that code to introduce the headset to your computer. It then relays the messages that set up a direct, encrypted WebRTC connection between them, and steps aside.
  • The service therefore handles the code and connection metadata: the network addresses (IP addresses and ports) of the two devices, contained in the WebRTC set-up messages, and the time of the connection. A pairing code expires after 5 minutes unless it is used. A reconnection code, which lets the headset reconnect after a short drop, expires after 12 hours. The service keeps them only in memory.
  • The service's log records when a computer or a headset connects, with its IP address and the outcome (for example "code expired"). It never records a code, a set-up message or a relay credential.
  • If a direct connection between your headset and your computer is impossible, the connection may pass through a relay (TURN) server run with the service. The relay forwards encrypted packets that it cannot read.

The loader web page

  • The app opens the loader page from vr.visivolab.oact.inaf.it. Like any web server, ours records each request in its access log: IP address, time, the address requested, and the browser's identification string.
  • On the headset, the page keeps a copy of its own files and of the VR page's files (a browser cache), so that later visits load faster. It also keeps the reconnection code for the current browser session. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes both.
  • The page sets no cookies and loads nothing from any other website.

Your data and your backend

  • The data you look at in VR come from the VisIVO backend that your copy of VisIVO Visual Analytics uses. This is your own computer, or a server of your institution or your computing centre. They travel over the encrypted WebRTC connection between that backend and your headset, and never through INAF's loader or pairing service.
  • To show you views of your data, the headset sends your backend what it needs to draw them: the position and orientation of the headset and of your controllers or hands, and the actions you take in VR. These go only to your backend and are not kept by the VR page.
  • When the backend is run by someone else (your institution, a computing centre), that operator's own policies apply to it.

What we do not do

  • No user accounts, no names, email addresses or contacts are asked for.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking or third-party software development kits.
  • No data is sold or shared with anyone, and none is used to identify you.

Meta, as the provider of the headset and of the Meta Horizon Store, processes data under its own privacy policy, independently of this app.

Why, and for how long

The connection metadata and logs above are processed only to connect your headset to your computer and to keep the service secure and working (for example, to limit abuse). This is in INAF's legitimate interest in running a public research service. Logs are kept only as long as needed for that and are then deleted by the server's routine log rotation.

Children

VisIVO VR is a scientific tool for researchers and students. It is not directed at children.

Your rights and contact

Under the EU General Data Protection Regulation you may ask for access to, correction or deletion of personal data about you, or object to its processing. Because we hold no account and keep no camera data, there is usually nothing linked to you. Server logs contain only IP addresses for a limited time. To ask a question or make a request, contact VisIVOLab through visivolab.oact.inaf.it or open an issue at github.com/VisIVOLab/VisIVOVisualAnalytics/issues. INAF's data protection officer can be reached through inaf.it. You may also lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali).

Changes

If what the app or the service does with data changes, this page is updated and its date above changes with it. The source of this page, of the app and of the services is public in the VisIVO Visual Analytics repository.
